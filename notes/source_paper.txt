Powerful solutions of σ(n) = 2σ∗(n):
a structural reduction of an OEIS conjecture

Working notes

June 2026

Abstract

Let σ be the sum-of-divisors function and σ∗ the sum-of-unitary-divisors function. We study

the conjecture (R. Stephan, “Prove or disprove” #35; OEIS A063880) that

σ(n) = 2σ∗(n) =⇒ n ≡ 108

(mod 216).

After reducing to a statement about powerful numbers, we record a body of results that com-
pletely settle several sub-cases and isolate the remaining difficulty to a single precise question.
The centrepiece is an exact identity, R(22)R(33) = 2, which collapses the conjecture to two
local conditions on the prime factorisation. We prove the two-prime case outright, settle the
6 | n case up to one explicit residual equation, and establish 2-adic, cyclotomic, and abundancy
constraints. We then locate the open kernel exactly: it is the inability to bound ω(n), the same
obstruction that has kept unitary perfect numbers open since 1966 — though, unlike those
problems, ours carries a convergent heuristic predicting finiteness.

7 Summary

Proven shell. The two-prime case (Theorem 3); the collapse reducing the Crux to “v2 = 2 and
v3 = 3” (Theorem 2); the case 6 | m up to the residual R(M ) = 100
91 , itself resolved for
ω(M ) ≤ 2 and verified empty below ∼ 1016 (Theorem 4); the 2-adic identity (Theorem 6);
cyclotomic entanglement (Theorem 7); and abundancy bounds (Proposition 8).

Open kernel. Powerful solutions with 6 ∤ m (forced to have ≥ 7 prime factors if coprime to 6),
together with the ω ≥ 3 residual. This kernel is of odd-perfect / unitary-perfect grade: the
entire gap is a bound on ω(m), which no current method supplies.

Belief. Theorem 9 plus the convergent heuristic make finiteness — and indeed uniqueness of 108

— overwhelmingly likely; what is missing is a proof, not evidence.
